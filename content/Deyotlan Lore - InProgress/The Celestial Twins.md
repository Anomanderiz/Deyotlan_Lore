# The Celestial Twins

The **Celestial Twins** are the closest thing Quil na Sko possesses to a unifying authority, though even that description may impose more order upon the fey than truly exists. The Twins appear as conjoined twins, one seemingly male and one seemingly female. In truth, each is understood by the fey as neither and both at once. **Sister Dark** is associated with winter, dolour and night and **Brother Day** is associated with summer, daylight and joy; between them they divide the many-hued domains of the furtive spring that emerges from winter and the well-sated autumn that fades into it.

## What They Are

The Twins are revered for their profound connection to nature and to the veil between life and death. If the fey listened to anyone at all, it would be the Twins – but this reverence takes many forms and few of them are straightforward obedience. The Twins, simply put, seem to be the only force capable of corralling the fey into collective action of any sort; gathering them at the equinoxes at Liss Moor to make important decisions.

They appear capable of reason, but their reasoning is inscrutable and their choices often seem to respond not to the world as it presently exists but to the branching possibilities around it. Therefore, while the Celestial Twins represent a semblance of order in the forest, they also represent the fey at their most capricious and volatile.

## The Celestial Court

At each equinox, the scattered fey gather beneath the Twins in the **Liss Moor**.

The Court is parliament only in the loosest sense, it is also pilgrimage, funeral, wedding feast, omen-reading and bacchanalia: a time of births and deaths, mourning and revelry, prophecies, bargains, reunions and old feuds.

Most collective action undertaken by Quil na Sko occurs at the behest of the Twins, this includes the rare occasions on which dwarven armies have been allowed through the forest unharmed. Whether the Twins command such acts or merely reveal that they should occur is a distinction that matters, perhaps, only to the fey.

## The Mortal Wish

An old dwarven belief holds that a mortal who reaches the Celestial Court when it gathers at Liss Moor on an equinox and looks upon the Twins may be granted the **one wish their heart most desires**.

Each equinox, dwarven pilgrims brave perilous fairy paths through the forest that their own families warned them never to walk. Some seek wealth or vengeance, some carry sick children and still others seek a lost lover or to hear the voice of a dead parent.

All know that those who are successful get their heart's desire but are never truly the same again and none speak of what they saw in the woods.
