# Deyotlan

![[Deyonahua.png]]

## Design Influence

**Deyotlan**, commonly rendered the **Realm of the Sun**, is a dwarven empire drawing chiefly upon Mexica and broader Nahua political and aesthetic influences with monumental stone cities, great public plazas and stepped architecture built from competing pseudo-city-states, while the fey of [[Quil na Sko]] (Forest of Shadows in _sheerack_) use Celtic-inspired names, albeit phonetically written.

The central contrast of [[Deyonahua]] (the landmass; 'the Sun-kissed world' in _deynalli_) is philosophical as much as political. The dwarves seek permanence, legacy and continuity while the fey exist comfortably amid death, decay, renewal and change; the dwarves seek to build against the tides of time while the fey accept that it washes away everything.

## Background

To foreign courts, Deyonahua is a single empire ruled by the dwarven **Huey Tlatoani (Great Speaker)**, with the fey of Quil na Sko as vassals and it serves as a useful diplomatic fiction.

In practice, the forest has never been conquered; its freeholds and salons obey their own customs, and the fey recognise no dwarven sovereign.

Yet, neither people can dispense with the other: the dwarves protect the forest from invasion, exploitation and dangers that require disciplined force while the fey tame Deyonahua's immense natural energy, temper the consequences of dwarven industry and maintain workings of land, weather and the occult that no dwarf can fathom.

Their coexistence is therefore not a perfect friendship, but interdependence and centuries of myth, bargains, superstition and mutual suspicion.

See [[Dwarves and Fey]].

## Peoples

### The Dwarves of Deyotlan

The dwarves are a stout people of stone, lineages and monuments; they inhabit both plains and mountains, raising cities, roads, fortresses, and glorious tombs intended to survive their builders by centuries if not millennia. A dwarven life only acquires meaning through what remains after it.

Tradition carries enormous moral weight in Deyotlan and laws accumulate precedents rather than being discarded or reworked. Crafts and debts in the lands of the empire pass through generations and old names of people and places are revered rather than forgotten or replaced.

There is only one sanctioned exception to this reverence for continuity: **Huey Tlatoani, the Great Speaker**. The sovereign alone possesses the sole right to force the whole civilisation to change. The ruling dynasty is, therefore, famously volatile; only the most dynamic of its children are likely to seize the throne.

Deyotlan itself is a union of ancient **altepetl** – powerful city-polities ruled by dynastic **Great Tecpan** ('Great Houses' in _deynalli_). Each Great Tecpan possesses an old and legitimate claim to the paramount throne. Three ancient Great Tecpan rule the three principal dwarven cities;. the current ruling clan is **[[Tecpan Olin]]**, the House of Motion, seated at [[Yollotlan]] ('Place of the Heart' in _deynalli_). **Tecpan Tecpatl** (House of Flint) rules the city of **Iztipec** ('Obsidian Hill' in _deynalli_), and **Tecpan Cuatli** ('House of the Eagle' in _deynalli_) rules **Tlecalco** ('Home of Fire' in _deynalli_).

Maintenance of the great port of **Acalpan** ('Place of Boats' in _deynalli_) as well as the formidable fortress at the border shared with Auridon – **Chimaltipan** ('Shield Mountain' in _deynalli_) – are chief amongst the duties of the Great Speaker.

See [[The Succession]].

### The Fey of Quil na Sko, the Forest of Shadows

The fey form no conventional kingdom, instead, they dwell in loosely confederated freeholds, salons, and wandering courts – gathering in appreciable numbers at the great stone henge of **Liss Moor** ('The Great Enclosure' in _sheerack_) only at the equinoxes.

They live close to the veil between life and death and treat mortality as a sage companion to life. Their witches commune with the dead, alter the course of fate and work rites across the boundaries of what was, what is and what may yet be.

The great **fey portal** deep in the heart of the Forest is a scar in the fabric of the world, providing passage between realms. It is this feature of the land of Deyonahua that has blessed it with verdancy never-ending; everything ever grows, always.

While blessed with supernatural abundance, the reminder of the Heroes March and the horrors from beyond, as well as the forest itself perennially encroaching upon settlements, brews a simmering resentment between dwarves and fey. The periodic horrors unleashed from the forest only serve to amplify concerns about the cultural practices of the fey and the powers with which they consort.

The dwarves have consequently spent centuries imagining them as psychopomps, banshees and thieves of children. While these stories are not entirely false, they are incomplete and misunderstood.

See [[Quil na Sko]] and [[The Celestial Twins]].

## Cultures

Dwarven and fey life has become deeply entangled despite the stories each tells about the other:

Dwarves brave the fairy paths through the forest at the equinoxes in the hope that one who looks upon the Celestial Twins may be granted the single wish their heart most desires. The sick seek fey witches when dwarven physicians have exhausted their remedies. Fey witches attend the births of every child of the Great Speaker and the **tlatoani** ('speaker' in _deynalli_; the rulers of the cities), standing beside dwarven physicians where they may guide mother and child through converging fates to the one most desired.

The dependence runs in the other direction as well; Quil na Sko is the most volatile place in Deyonahua, where the veil between worlds is perilously thin. Fey rites sometimes loose things their makers can no longer command for, no witchcraft may undo what witchcraft has wrought. On such moonless nights, pale ravens fly from the forest toward the mountains to summon **[[Tecuani Tamanime]]** the Wild Hunt – disciplined dwarven hunting companies sent to destroy creatures that should never have crossed the veil.

The dwarves are, in this sense, the calm eye of Deyonahua's raging natural storm.

See [[Customs of Deyonahua]] and [[Tecuani Tamanime]].

## Current Affairs

The approaching coronation of the Archon of [[Endurath]] has brought the old ambiguity of Deyonahua dangerously close to boiling point.

The Great Speaker has requested that three seers of Quil na Sko accompany his retinue. While abroad, they will appear to be fey vassals answering the summons of their sovereign, but the fey understand the matter quite differently: they have accepted a request from an esteemed neighbour and travel beside the imperial embassy, not as vassals beneath its banners.

Each sides fears what the other may do with special friendship to the Archon. The dwarven court worries that the seers may cultivate an independent relationship with Endurath and weaken the Great Speaker's claim to speak for all Deyonahua while the fey fear that the Great Speaker will seek formal recognition of his nominal sovereignty over the entire landmass, turning a useful political fiction into fact.

The coronation may reaffirm the arrangement that has endured for centuries and seen the land flourish under these two proud peoples – or it may bring it to ruin.

See [[The Coronation of Endurath]].

## Relationships

At the heart of Deyonahua is a contradiction neither side has wished to resolve: the empire claims a forest it neither controls nor understands.

The dwarves provide armies and the stubborn sanity needed when the supernatural excesses of the land become dangerous. The fey make the country itself habitable, tempering storms, floods and the ecological wounds of dwarven mines and ever-burning forges.

In the past, when enemies have come from the sea, the fey have lifted the veils between worlds so dwarven armies could cross the forest in impossible time to access the vast coast. Whenever something terrible escapes the control of the fey, dwarves have always answered the call, braving the terrible forces of the world beyond to save the forest.

Similarly, when plague enters a dwarven city, those who warn their children about fairy witches carry those same children into the forest by night, to be touched by one of the Eeshee ('People of the Grave Mounds, in _sheerack_) and so be cured.

Crucially, the discovery of potion-making by dwarves from the House of the Eagle at Tlecalco, created an economic interdependence between the fey and the dwarves. The prized fruits that are used in the making of these arcane concoctions only grow in the mystical Forest of Shadows. The potion trade brings in enormous wealth to the dwarves, who in turn raise vast armies to protect the Forest from would-be invaders.

Neither people is comfortable admitting how sorely it needs the other. They fear one another, resent one another and misunderstand one another, but they also recognise – deep in their hearts – that they know one another better than either knows any foreign power.
